Authorship Guidelines

General Guidelines:

Establishing authorship early is crucial for several reasons, especially in collaborative research projects:

1. Avoids Conflicts: Clearly defining who will be credited and in what order prevents misunderstandings and disputes later on.

2. Aligns Contributions: Ensuring that each author’s role is acknowledged helps clarify expectations about responsibilities and workload.

3. Facilitates Transparency: Early discussions promote fairness and adherence to ethical authorship guidelines (e.g., ICMJE or CRediT taxonomy).

4. Enhances Collaboration: When roles and contributions are clear, team members can focus on the research rather than navigating authorship disputes..

6. Complies with Journal Requirements: Many journals require authorship agreements before submission, so clarifying it early prevents delays.

Reminders:

  • Contributions and order must be explicitly discussed before publication.
  • Order of authors may be specific to a publication venue and discipline.
  • Project leads are the individual(s) who manage project and contribution responsibilities.
  • Project leads are designated at the start of the process.
  • Roles and responsibilities may change throughout the publication preparation process.
  • The final order may differ from the initial one based on the changing roles and responsibilities.
  • When possible and necessary, designate equal contributions of leading authors.
  • Explicitly discuss what contributions merit a co-authorship vs a citation (e.g., a previously published dataset or software) versus an acknowledgement.
  • All data and software should be used with appropriate citation (see data sources template) and follow the imageomics digital products release and licensing policy.
  • Designs experiments and/or generates data.
  • Data verification, reproducibility.
  • Be sure to Acknowledge use of AI/ChatGPT if used in writing paper.
