Educational Tools & Resources

Providing educational tools and resources for the Imageomics Institute is paramount to its mission of advancing knowledge and expertise in the field of imageomics.

These resources serve as the backbone for training the next generation of scientists, researchers, and professionals. By offering accessible and comprehensive educational materials, such as online courses, workshops, and specialized software, the Imageomics Institute ensures that individuals from a range of backgrounds can develop the skills needed to harness the power of AI and ML for transformative research. These resources not only empower students and scholars but also foster collaboration and innovation, ultimately driving advancements in the understanding of complex biological systems and their applications in areas like medicine, biology, and beyond. In equipping individuals with the necessary tools and knowledge, the Imageomics Institute plays a pivotal role in shaping the future of imageomics research and its broader impact on scientific discovery.

Educational Tools & Resources

Experiential Introduction to Imageomics Course

Imageomics is a new, transdiscipline that unites theory and methods from analytical and biological fields to advance knowledge via image data.  Genes are to Genomics, as images are to Imageomics. In 2022, the team at  the Imageomics Institute ran a course that introduced Imageomics, methods for analyzing imageomic data, and prepared students for imageomics projects. This course included students from many universities across multiple continents. This course was a three-part series that took place over the course of an academic year.  Part 1 was virtual. Part 2 was a field course held in Kenya and Part 3 was a project-based class during which students used data collected during the field portion to answer research questions using methods learning in Part I.  Students who enrolled in Fall 2022 committed to participating in Spring 2023.   

Experiential Introduction to AI & Ecology Course

This was a year-long interdisciplinary, project-driven course that brought together biologists, computer scientists, and those from related disciplines to learn, collaborate, and answer questions in ecology/biology/biodiversity science using computational machine learning (ML) approaches. The course begins with foundational concepts in science and analytic areas while formulating group-project ideas, includes a field experience for collecting data, and ends with completing  group projects asynchronously and receiving professional development synchronously.  The goal of the course is to conduct interdisciplinary scientific inquiry and research at the intersection of AI and ecology, collaborate with scientists across disciplines, become immersed in the research methods of different fields, and explore how the collaboration between those disciplines results in the emergence of new ideas, approaches, and discoveries. 

QUEST Teacher Training Program at Princeton University

The Imageomics Institute has partnered with Princeton University on their QUEST Teacher Training Program since 2022. QUEST institutes have been held each summer for over 30 years and are designed to enhance teachers' knowledge of science and mathematics through laboratory experiments and experiences aligned with the Next Generation Science Standards. The program is co-taught by current researchers and teachers to support the development of teachers’ skills to apply science/math content knowledge in their classroom and increases teachers’ enthusiasm, knowledge and confidence in teaching science and math.

TDAI Data Science Summer Camp

The Imageomics Institute partners with the Translational Data Analytics Institute to host a free summer camp designed for students in rising grades 7, 8, and 9 to explore data science and analytics. It is an exciting, fast-growing field centered on scientific methods and processes for extracting new knowledge from data. Students who learn these skills can combine them with an unimaginably wide range of interests, from business and biology to health care and education, to city planning and social services. Over the course of five days in the summer, campers will:

  • Work in student teams and with mentors to develop analytical and problem-solving skills
  • Learn from scientists and students across the data spectrum
  • Explore educational and career opportunities in data science and analytics

Imageomics Peer Alliance for Learning and Support (PALS) Program

The Peer Alliance for Learning and Support (PALS) Program promotes interdisciplinary capacity building among NextGen community members through a reciprocal peer mentoring relationship focused on shared knowledge transfer, skill development, and relationship building. Each participating NextGen will be provided with a PAL in a different discipline (i.e., computer scientists will be paired with biologists, and vice versa) who they can reach out to when they need guidance on a particular imageomics-related research problem/task or when navigating difficult situations with other researchers or faculty.
