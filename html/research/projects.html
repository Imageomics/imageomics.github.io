Our approach

Knowledge from biological ontologies, leveraging the structure they impose, together with multiple imaging formats and other data, fuels the development of the next generation of Knowledge-Guided Machine Learning models.

These models are more interpretable, transferable, robust, label-efficient, and sample-efficient—expanding the role of machine learning in addressing the most challenging biology problems and forming a virtuous cycle.

Behavioral Traits from Videos

Overview

To analyze the behavior of baboons, zebras, and giraffes through the application of computer vision techniques by utilizing videos that were captured using drones in Kenya. With computer vision algorithms, the team is able to recognize a range of distinct behavioral patterns exhibited by the animals, including activities such as walking, grazing, auto-grooming, mutual grooming, trotting, running, drinking, herding, lying down, mounting-mating, sniffing, urinating, defecating, dusting, fighting, chasing, and more. This project highlights the potential of computer vision and artificial intelligence to enhance our understanding of animal behavior in the wild.

Overview

To understand the heritability of physical patterns, for instance spots in leopards or stripes in zebras. The approach leverages computer vision methods to quantify similarity between patterns and to align those with known pedigrees to understand genetic relatedness and pattern similarity.
